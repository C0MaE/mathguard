To find the magnetic flux density at a distance $r = 5\,\mathrm{cm}$ from the wire, we can use the formula for the magnetic field due to a long straight wire:

B = μ0 \* I / (2 \* π \* r)

where B is the magnetic flux density, μ0 is the magnetic constant, I is the current, and r is the distance from the wire.

Given values:
I = 10 A
r = 5 cm = 0.05 m
μ0 = 1.25664 \* 10^(-6) N/A^2

Plugging in the values, we get:

B = μ0 \* I / (2 \* π \* r)
= (1.25664 \* 10^(-6) N/A^2) \* (10 A) / (2 \* π \* 0.05 m)
= (1.25664 \* 10^(-6) N/A^2) \* (10 A) / (0.31416 m)
= 1.006 \* 10^(-3) T

So, the magnetic flux density at a distance $r = 5\,\mathrm{cm}$ from the wire is:

RESULT
B [magnetic flux density] = B = 1.006 \* 10^(-3) T
