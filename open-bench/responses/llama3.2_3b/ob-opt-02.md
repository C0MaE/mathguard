To solve this problem, we need to use Snell's law, which relates the angles of incidence and refraction to the refractive indices of the two media. Snell's law is given by:

n1 sin(θ1) = n2 sin(θ2)

where n1 and n2 are the refractive indices of the two media, and θ1 and θ2 are the angles of incidence and refraction, respectively.

In this case, we want to find the critical angle, which is the angle of incidence above which the light is totally reflected. This occurs when the angle of refraction is 90 degrees (since the light is not refracted, but rather reflected).

We can rearrange Snell's law to solve for θ1:

sin(θ1) = (n2 / n1) sin(90°)

Since sin(90°) = 1, we get:

sin(θ1) = n2 / n1

Now, we can plug in the values for n1 and n2:

sin(θ1) = 1.0 / 1.5

sin(θ1) = 0.67

θ1 = arcsin(0.67)

θ1 ≈ 41.8°

Therefore, the critical angle is approximately 41.8 degrees.
