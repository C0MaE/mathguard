To solve this problem, we can use the formula for the work done by an ideal gas during an isothermal expansion:

W = nRT ln(V2/V1)

where:
- W = work done by the gas
- n = number of moles of gas
- R = gas constant
- T = temperature in Kelvin
- V1 = initial volume
- V2 = final volume

Given values:
- n = 2 mol
- R = 8.314 J/(mol K)
- T = 300 K
- V1 = 10 L
- V2 = 30 L

First, we calculate the natural logarithm of the ratio of the final volume to the initial volume:

ln(V2/V1) = ln(30/10) = ln(3) = 1.0986

Now, we can plug in the values to the formula:

W = nRT ln(V2/V1)
= 2 mol * 8.314 J/(mol K) * 300 K * 1.0986
= 2 * 8.314 * 300 * 1.0986
= 5479.51 J

Therefore, the work done by the gas is:

RESULT
W [work done by the gas] = 5479.51 J
