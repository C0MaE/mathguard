To solve this problem, we can use the principle of conservation of momentum. The momentum of the rocket before and after the burn can be calculated as follows:

Initial momentum: $p_0 = m_0 \cdot 0 = 0\,\mathrm{kg\,m/s}$

After the burn, the momentum of the rocket is $p_1 = m_1 \cdot v_1$, where $v_1$ is the speed of the rocket after the burn. The momentum of the exhaust is $p_{\mathrm{exh}} = m_0 - m_1 \cdot u$, where $u$ is the exhaust speed.

Since the total momentum is conserved, we can set up the following equation:

$$p_0 + p_{\mathrm{exh}} = p_1$$

Substituting the expressions for $p_0$, $p_1$, and $p_{\mathrm{exh}}$, we get:

$$0 + (m_0 - m_1 \cdot u) = m_1 \cdot v_1$$

Simplifying and solving for $v_1$, we get:

$$v_1 = \frac{m_0 - m_1 \cdot u}{m_1}$$

Now, we can plug in the given values:

$$v_1 = \frac{1000\,\mathrm{kg} - 400\,\mathrm{kg} \cdot 3000\,\mathrm{m/s}}{400\,\mathrm{kg}}$$

$$v_1 = \frac{1000\,\mathrm{kg} - 1200000\,\mathrm{kg\,m/s}}{400\,\mathrm{kg}}$$

$$v_1 = \frac{-1190000\,\mathrm{kg\,m/s}}{400\,\mathrm{kg}}$$

$$v_1 = -2975\,\mathrm{m/s}$$

Since the rocket is moving in the opposite direction of the exhaust, the speed of the rocket is the absolute value of $v_1$:

$$v_1 = 2975\,\mathrm{m/s}$$

The gain in speed is:

$$\mathrm{dv} = v_1 - 0 = 2975\,\mathrm{m/s}$$

RESULT
dv [gain in speed] = 2975 m/s
