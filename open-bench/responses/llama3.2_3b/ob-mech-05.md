To solve this problem, we need to follow these steps:

1. Calculate the potential energy stored in the spring when it is compressed by $d = 10\,\mathrm{cm}$.
2. Use the potential energy to find the initial velocity of the ball when it is launched upwards.
3. Calculate the maximum height the ball reaches above the point where it leaves the spring.

Step 1: Calculate the potential energy stored in the spring

The potential energy stored in a spring is given by:

U = (1/2)kxd^2

where U is the potential energy, k is the spring constant, x is the compression distance, and d is the displacement.

Substituting the given values:

U = (1/2) × 200 × 0.1^2
U = 1 J

Step 2: Calculate the initial velocity of the ball

The potential energy stored in the spring is converted into kinetic energy of the ball as it is launched upwards. We can set up the following equation:

U = (1/2)mv^2

where U is the potential energy, m is the mass of the ball, and v is the initial velocity.

Rearranging the equation to solve for v:

v = √(2U/m)
v = √(2 × 1 / 0.05)
v = √(40)
v = 6.32 m/s

Step 3: Calculate the maximum height the ball reaches

The maximum height the ball reaches is given by:

h = (v^2) / (2g)

where h is the maximum height, v is the initial velocity, and g is the acceleration due to gravity.

Substituting the values:

h = (6.32^2) / (2 × 9.81)
h = 40.05 / 19.62
h = 2.04 m
