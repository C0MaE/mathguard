To solve this problem, we can use the formula for the radius of a circular path in a magnetic field:

rc = mv / (qB)

where:
- rc is the radius of the circle
- m is the mass of the proton
- v is the velocity of the proton
- q is the charge of the proton
- B is the magnetic field strength

Given values:
m = 1.6726 × 10^(-27) kg
v = 10^6 m/s
q = 1.602 × 10^(-19) C
B = 0.5 T

Plugging in the values, we get:

rc = (1.6726 × 10^(-27) kg) × (10^6 m/s) / ((1.602 × 10^(-19) C) × (0.5 T))

First, calculate the numerator:
(1.6726 × 10^(-27) kg) × (10^6 m/s) = 1.6726 × 10^(-21) kg·m/s

Then, calculate the denominator:
(1.602 × 10^(-19) C) × (0.5 T) = 8.01 × 10^(-20) C·T

Now, divide the numerator by the denominator:
rc = (1.6726 × 10^(-21) kg·m/s) / (8.01 × 10^(-20) C·T) = 0.2087 m

So, the radius of the proton's circular path is approximately 0.2087 meters.
