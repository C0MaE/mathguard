To determine the angle above which light undergoes **total internal reflection** when traveling from glass to air, we use the concept of the **critical angle**. This occurs when light is incident from a medium with a higher refractive index ($n_1$) to one with a lower refractive index ($n_2$), and the angle of incidence exceeds the critical angle.

---

### Step-by-Step Derivation

1. **Snell's Law**:  
   $$
   n_1 \sin(\theta_1) = n_2 \sin(\theta_2)
   $$
   At the **critical angle**, the refracted ray is at $90^\circ$, so $\sin(\theta_2) = 1$.

2. **Set up the critical angle equation**:  
   $$
   n_1 \sin(\theta_c) = n_2
   $$

3. **Solve for the critical angle**:  
   $$
   \sin(\theta_c) = \frac{n_2}{n_1}
   $$

4. **Take the inverse sine (arcsin)**:  
   $$
   \theta_c = \sin^{-1}\left(\frac{n_2}{n_1}\right)
   $$

---

### Plug in the Given Values

- $n_1 = 1.5$ (glass)
- $n_2 = 1.0$ (air)

$$
\theta_c = \sin^{-1}\left(\frac{1.0}{1.5}\right) = \sin^{-1}\left(\frac{2}{3}\right)
$$

$$
\theta_c \approx 41.8^\circ
$$

---

### Final Answer

RESULT  
theta [critical angle] = sin^{-1}(n_2/n_1) = 41.8 deg
