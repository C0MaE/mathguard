To determine the **maximum kinetic energy** of the emitted electrons in the photoelectric effect, we use **Einstein's photoelectric equation**:

$$
K_{\text{max}} = \frac{hc}{\lambda} - W_A
$$

Where:
- $ h = 6.626 \times 10^{-34} \, \mathrm{J \cdot s} $ (Planck's constant),
- $ c = 2.998 \times 10^8 \, \mathrm{m/s} $ (speed of light),
- $ \lambda = 250 \, \mathrm{nm} = 250 \times 10^{-9} \, \mathrm{m} $ (wavelength of incident light),
- $ W_A = 4.3 \, \mathrm{eV} $ (work function of the metal).

---

### Step 1: Use the shortcut for energy in eV·nm

A commonly used approximation is:

$$
hc \approx 1240 \, \mathrm{eV \cdot nm}
$$

This allows us to compute the energy of the photon directly in electron volts:

$$
E_{\text{photon}} = \frac{hc}{\lambda} = \frac{1240 \, \mathrm{eV \cdot nm}}{250 \, \mathrm{nm}} = 4.96 \, \mathrm{eV}
$$

---

### Step 2: Subtract the work function

$$
K_{\text{max}} = E_{\text{photon}} - W_A = 4.96 \, \mathrm{eV} - 4.3 \, \mathrm{eV} = 0.66 \, \mathrm{eV}
$$

---

### Final Answer

RESULT  
Ekin [maximum kinetic energy] = (hc/λ - W_A) = 0.66 eV
