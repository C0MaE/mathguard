To determine the frequency heard by a stationary observer when an ambulance is moving toward them, we apply the **Doppler effect** for a **moving source** and **stationary observer**. The general formula for this scenario is:

$$
f' = f_0 \cdot \frac{c}{c - v}
$$

Where:
- $ f_0 = 440\,\mathrm{Hz} $ is the emitted frequency,
- $ c = 343\,\mathrm{m/s} $ is the speed of sound,
- $ v = 30\,\mathrm{m/s} $ is the speed of the ambulance (source) moving toward the observer.

---

### Step-by-Step Calculation:

1. **Calculate the denominator**:
   $$
   c - v = 343\,\mathrm{m/s} - 30\,\mathrm{m/s} = 313\,\mathrm{m/s}
   $$

2. **Compute the observed frequency**:
   $$
   f' = 440\,\mathrm{Hz} \cdot \frac{343}{313}
   $$

3. **Perform the division**:
   $$
   \frac{343}{313} \approx 1.0958466
   $$

4. **Multiply by the original frequency**:
   $$
   f' = 440 \cdot 1.0958466 \approx 482.17\,\mathrm{Hz}
   $$

---

### Final Result:

$$
\boxed{f [observed frequency] = \frac{c}{c - v} \cdot f_0 = 482.17\,\mathrm{Hz}}
$$
