To determine the work delivered per cycle by a Carnot engine, we use the fundamental relationship between heat input, work output, and efficiency. The efficiency of a Carnot engine is defined as:

$$
\eta = 1 - \frac{T_c}{T_h}
$$

Where:
- $ T_h = 600\,\mathrm{K} $ is the temperature of the hot reservoir,
- $ T_c = 300\,\mathrm{K} $ is the temperature of the cold reservoir.

The work $ W $ delivered per cycle is related to the heat input $ Q_h $ by the efficiency:

$$
W = Q_h \cdot \eta = Q_h \cdot \left(1 - \frac{T_c}{T_h}\right)
$$

Substituting the given values:

$$
W = 1000\,\mathrm{J} \cdot \left(1 - \frac{300}{600}\right) = 1000\,\mathrm{J} \cdot \left(1 - 0.5\right) = 1000\,\mathrm{J} \cdot 0.5 = 500\,\mathrm{J}
$$

---

**RESULT**  
W [work per cycle] = $ Q_h \cdot \left(1 - \frac{T_c}{T_h}\right) $ = 500 J
