To determine how high the ball rises above the point where it leaves the spring, we apply the principle of energy conservation. The spring stores elastic potential energy when compressed, and this energy is converted into kinetic energy as the ball is launched. As the ball rises, its kinetic energy is then converted into gravitational potential energy. Since air resistance is neglected, all the initial spring potential energy is converted into gravitational potential energy at the ball's maximum height.

---

### Step 1: Write the energy conservation equation

The spring potential energy is given by:

$$
E_{\text{spring}} = \frac{1}{2} k d^2
$$

At the maximum height, all of this energy is converted into gravitational potential energy:

$$
E_{\text{gravitational}} = m g h
$$

Setting these equal:

$$
\frac{1}{2} k d^2 = m g h
$$

---

### Step 2: Solve for the maximum height $ h $

$$
h = \frac{1}{2} \cdot \frac{k d^2}{m g}
$$

---

### Step 3: Plug in the given values

- $ k = 200\,\mathrm{N/m} $
- $ d = 10\,\mathrm{cm} = 0.1\,\mathrm{m} $
- $ m = 50\,\mathrm{g} = 0.05\,\mathrm{kg} $
- $ g = 9.81\,\mathrm{m/s^2} $

$$
h = \frac{1}{2} \cdot \frac{200 \cdot (0.1)^2}{0.05 \cdot 9.81}
$$

$$
h = \frac{1}{2} \cdot \frac{200 \cdot 0.01}{0.4905}
$$

$$
h = \frac{1}{2} \cdot \frac{2}{0.4905}
$$

$$
h = \frac{1}{0.4905} \approx 2.039\,\mathrm{m}
$$

---

### Final Result

$$
\text{RESULT}
$$
h [maximum height] = $\frac{1}{2} \cdot \frac{k d^2}{m g}$ = $2.04\,\mathrm{m}$
