To determine the **fundamental frequency** of a vibrating guitar string, we use the standard formula for the first harmonic (fundamental mode) of a string fixed at both ends:

$$
f = \frac{1}{2L} \sqrt{\frac{F}{\mu}}
$$

Where:
- $ f $ is the fundamental frequency (in Hz),
- $ L $ is the length of the string (in meters),
- $ F $ is the tension in the string (in Newtons),
- $ \mu $ is the linear mass density of the string (in kg/m).

---

### **Step 1: Convert Units to SI**

Given:
- $ L = 65\,\mathrm{cm} = 0.65\,\mathrm{m} $
- $ \mu = 5\,\mathrm{g/m} = 0.005\,\mathrm{kg/m} $
- $ F = 70\,\mathrm{N} $

---

### **Step 2: Plug Values into the Formula**

$$
f = \frac{1}{2 \times 0.65} \sqrt{\frac{70}{0.005}}
$$

$$
f = \frac{1}{1.3} \sqrt{14000}
$$

$$
f = \frac{1}{1.3} \times 118.32
$$

$$
f \approx 91.02\,\mathrm{Hz}
$$

---

### **Final Result**

$$
\text{RESULT}
f \text{ [fundamental frequency]} = \frac{1}{2L} \sqrt{\frac{F}{\mu}} = 91\,\mathrm{Hz}
$$
