To determine the energy stored in a parallel-plate capacitor, we use the formula:

$$
W = \frac{1}{2} C V^2
$$

where:
- $ W $ is the stored energy,
- $ C $ is the capacitance of the capacitor,
- $ V $ is the voltage across the capacitor.

---

### Step 1: Calculate the Capacitance

The capacitance $ C $ of a parallel-plate capacitor in vacuum is given by:

$$
C = \varepsilon_0 \frac{A}{d}
$$

Given:
- $ \varepsilon_0 = 8.854 \times 10^{-12} \, \mathrm{F/m} $
- $ A = 0.01 \, \mathrm{m^2} $
- $ d = 1 \, \mathrm{mm} = 0.001 \, \mathrm{m} $

$$
C = 8.854 \times 10^{-12} \times \frac{0.01}{0.001} = 8.854 \times 10^{-12} \times 10 = 8.854 \times 10^{-11} \, \mathrm{F}
$$

---

### Step 2: Calculate the Stored Energy

Now, using the energy formula:

$$
W = \frac{1}{2} C V^2
$$

Given:
- $ V = 100 \, \mathrm{V} $

$$
W = \frac{1}{2} \times 8.854 \times 10^{-11} \times (100)^2 = \frac{1}{2} \times 8.854 \times 10^{-11} \times 10^4
$$

$$
W = \frac{1}{2} \times 8.854 \times 10^{-7} = 4.427 \times 10^{-7} \, \mathrm{J}
$$
